World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026

Two days of US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine in Abu Dhabi ended with agreement on the war's largest prisoner exchange but no ceasefire, with territory still the main obstacle; US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff head to Moscow on Thursday (Reuters; NPR). Russia struck six electricity substations in central Ukraine, and NATO jets were scrambled as drones strayed into Baltic airspace (Reuters).

Iran's negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said the Strait of Hormuz would "never return" to its pre-war state, and security chief Mohsen Rezaei said no enriched nuclear material would leave Iran (Al Jazeera). Iran also designated Argentina's navy and air force as terrorist organisations (Iranian Foreign Ministry).

At the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, UN chief Antonio Guterres warned that slow action on climate had put the world "on a path to destruction"; host Turkey is pushing a goal of 35% of global energy from electricity by 2035, while the US stayed away for a second year (Reuters; AFP).

In Gaza, Israeli strikes killed at least five Palestinians on Wednesday, and the UN said more than 150,000 aid tents were held outside Gaza awaiting Israeli approval after floods swamped 70,000 tents (Al Jazeera; UN).

A 41-year-old US Army soldier was arrested in Gloucester, England, on suspicion of spying for Russia (BBC). Sudan's army said it shot down two drones in West Kordofan and East Darfur, taking its reported tally to 29 (SUNA).

Wall Street rallied on strong CoreWeave earnings: the S&P 500 rose 1.3% to 7,619.38, the Nasdaq 1.6% to 25,466.19 and the Dow 0.5% to 52,486.49; Brent crude rose to $92.76 and gold slipped below $4,000 (AP). Anthropic set a $70-$78 price range for its IPO, which could raise about $64 billion (company filing). IBM announced a quantum processor designed for 520 programmable qubits (IBM).

Twelve people died in two fog pile-ups on Bulgaria's Trakia motorway (BBC).
